Overview

What technical SEO services cover, and what they do not

Technical SEO is the foundation layer of search. It covers how search engines discover your URLs, whether they can render the content, which version of a page they index and how quickly pages load for real users. It does not replace good content or a sensible site structure, but without it the best content can sit unindexed or split across duplicate URLs.

The technical issues I see most often

Important pages marked Crawled, currently not indexed or Discovered, currently not indexed in Search Console

Faceted navigation and URL parameters creating thousands of near-identical URLs

Canonical tags pointing to the wrong page, or conflicting with redirects and sitemaps

Redirect chains and internal links that point to redirected or broken URLs

JavaScript frameworks that render key content or links only in the browser

Slow Largest Contentful Paint from oversized images, render-blocking scripts or slow servers

Hreflang errors on multi-country sites

Structured data that is invalid, misleading or missing where it would help

Each of these has a known fix, but the right fix depends on the platform, the size of the site and what your developers can change.

That is why the deliverable is a prioritised plan rather than a raw crawl export.

How I prioritise technical fixes

A large site can have hundreds of technical warnings, and most tools flag them with equal urgency.

I score each issue by how many important URLs it affects, whether it blocks indexing or only adds friction, and how much effort it needs.

Indexing blockers on revenue pages come first

Cosmetic warnings that do not affect crawling or users come last, or are dropped.

Each item becomes a ticket with the affected URLs, the evidence, the expected outcome and a test your developers can run to confirm the fix.

That keeps engineering time focused and makes progress easy to verify.

JavaScript SEO and rendering

Modern sites built with React, Next.js, Vue or similar frameworks can rank well, but only if search engines receive the important content and links.

I check the raw HTML against the rendered page, test with the URL Inspection tool and look for content that only appears after user interaction. Server-side rendering or static generation for key templates usually solves the problem; client-side rendering alone is a common cause of thin indexing.

Site migrations without traffic loss

Redesigns, domain changes, platform moves and URL restructures are the moments when organic traffic is most at risk.

A safe migration needs a complete URL inventory, a one-to-one redirect map, checks on staging before launch, and close monitoring of crawl errors, indexing and rankings for several weeks afterwards. I plan and QA migrations alongside your developers, or review a plan your team has already written.

Core Web Vitals and page experience

Core Web Vitals measure loading (LCP), responsiveness (INP) and visual stability (CLS) for real users.

Google uses page experience signals as part of its ranking systems, but relevance and helpfulness matter more, so speed work is prioritised where pages are genuinely slow or unstable. Typical fixes include compressing and correctly sizing images, preloading the main image, reducing unused JavaScript, setting dimensions on media and improving server response times.
