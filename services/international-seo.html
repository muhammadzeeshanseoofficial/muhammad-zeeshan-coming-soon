International SEO for brands expanding into new countries and languages

International SEO helps businesses rank in more than one country or language, with the right site structure, correct hreflang, real localisation and keyword research for each market.

Overview

What International SEO solves

When a site serves several countries or languages, search engines need to know which version to show to which user.

Without clear signals, the US page may rank in the UK, the English page may outrank the French one in France, or near-identical country pages may be treated as duplicates.

International SEO sets up the structure and signals that prevent this.

Choosing a site structure

The main options are country-code domains (example.fr), subdomains (fr.example.com) and subfolders (example.com/fr/).

Google supports all three.

Subfolders keep authority on one domain and are easiest to maintain; country domains send the strongest local signal but need separate authority building.

The choice depends on budget, legal needs and how independently each market operates.

Hreflang done correctly

Hreflang annotations tell Google about alternate versions of a page for different languages or regions.

They must be reciprocal, use valid language and region codes, point to canonical URLs and include an x-default where appropriate. Hreflang errors are among the most common international issues, and I audit them across sitemaps, HTML and headers.

Localisation, not just translation

People in different countries search differently, even in the same language: holiday versus vacation, mobile phone versus cell phone.

Prices, currencies, delivery details, legal information, examples and trust signals also differ. Localised pages answer local needs, which is why separate keyword research per market is part of every engagement.

Avoiding automatic redirects by location

Redirecting users automatically based on IP address can stop search engines from seeing all versions, since crawlers often come from the US.

A better pattern is to suggest the local version with a banner while letting users and crawlers access every page.

Market prioritisation

Expanding into every country at once spreads resources thin

I help assess search demand, competition and business readiness for each market, then plan a phased rollout.

Markets I commonly work on include the US, UK, Canada, Australia, the UAE, France and Italy, with dedicated pages for each, such as the UK and UAE market pages.

Multilingual content quality

Machine translation without review can produce awkward or wrong content that undermines trust.

Translations should be reviewed by fluent speakers, and key pages adapted rather than literally translated. Each language version should be complete enough to stand on its own.

Local authority in each market

Rankings in a new country also depend on local relevance and authority: links and mentions from local publications, local business directories and partnerships.

Link building plans are adapted per market rather than relying only on the main domain's authority.

Technical checks for international sites

Beyond hreflang, I check canonical tags across versions, language declarations in HTML, sitemaps per market, currency and structured data per country, and performance for users far from your servers.

Search Console properties or filters per market make performance easier to track.

A typical International SEO engagement

It starts with an audit of the current setup: structure, hreflang, duplicate content, crawling and performance per market.

Next comes keyword research and content planning for each priority market, followed by implementation support for your developers and translators.

After launch, I monitor indexing and rankings per country and refine the plan as data arrives.

Each market gets its own targets, because competition and demand vary widely.

Common International SEO mistakes

Translating the site but leaving titles, URLs and image alt text in English; setting every page's canonical to the main language version; using flags instead of language names in selectors; blocking country versions in robots.txt; and launching many markets with thin content are the problems I see most.

Each weakens the signals that help the right version rank in the right country.

Languages and countries in the same market

Some countries have several languages, such as Canada, Switzerland or Belgium, and some languages span many countries, such as Arabic, French and Spanish.

Hreflang can target a language alone or a language-country combination, and the right choice depends on whether your offer changes by country.
