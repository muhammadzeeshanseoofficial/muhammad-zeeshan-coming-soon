People-first content, as Google describes it

Google's helpful content guidance asks whether content is created primarily for people, demonstrates first-hand experience or expertise, gives a satisfying answer and would be worth bookmarking or sharing.

Content written only to target keywords, summarising what others say without adding anything, or produced at scale with little oversight tends to perform poorly and can fall under the scaled content abuse policy.

SEO content services therefore start with what you know that others do not: your processes, customer questions, results, opinions and examples.

That is what makes content useful and difficult to copy.

Topic clusters and site structure

A topic cluster is a main page on a broad subject supported by detailed pages on related questions, all linked together.

Clusters help readers explore a topic and help search engines understand which page is the main authority. I plan clusters around your services so informational content supports the pages that bring in customers.

Briefs that make writing faster and better

A good brief states the target query and intent, the reader, the questions to answer, the angle that will make the piece better than what ranks now, internal links to include and any expert input needed.

Writers spend less time guessing and editors spend less time rewriting.

Refreshing content before writing more

Many sites have older content that ranked once and slipped

Updating facts, adding missing sections, merging overlapping posts and improving internal links often brings faster gains than publishing new articles. Every content engagement starts with an inventory of what already exists.

Using AI tools responsibly

AI tools can help with research, outlines and editing

Google's guidance focuses on the quality and purpose of content rather than how it was produced, but publishing unreviewed AI text at scale to manipulate rankings is spam. Every piece I deliver is reviewed for accuracy, original insight and clarity by a person.

Content for AI search and featured answers

Clear headings, direct answers near the top of sections, accurate facts and clear authorship make content easier for search features and AI assistants to understand and cite.

This overlaps with AI search optimization, which covers brand visibility in assistants more broadly.

Measuring content performance

Each piece is tracked for impressions, clicks, rankings and its contribution to enquiries or sales, either directly or through internal links to service pages.

Content that underperforms after a fair period is improved, merged or retired, so the site stays focused.

Content works best on a site with sound technical foundations and well-optimised existing pages.

Showing experience and expertise on the page

Readers and search engines both look for signs that content comes from someone who knows the subject.

That means named authors with relevant backgrounds, first-hand photos and examples, clear dates of publication and review, explanations of how recommendations were reached and honest statements of limitations.

For topics that affect health, money or safety, expert review is essential.

These signals are built into every brief and every article I deliver.

A typical content engagement

Month one covers the content inventory, topic research and the cluster plan.

From month two, a steady schedule of new pieces and refreshes begins, agreed with you in advance.

Each month you receive the published pieces, a short performance update and the plan for the next month.

The volume is set by what your budget and subject experts can support, because a few excellent pieces outperform many average ones.

Content plans can also be combined with local SEO for location-based businesses or e-commerce SEO for category and buying-guide content.
