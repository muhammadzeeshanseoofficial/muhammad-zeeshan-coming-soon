E-commerce & Shopify SEO

Ecommerce SEO that grows organic revenue for online stores

Ecommerce SEO helps online stores rank category and product pages for the searches shoppers make, fixes the technical problems large catalogues create and turns organic visits into revenue.

Get a free SEO auditBook a call

Where ecommerce SEO makes the biggest difference

Most organic revenue for online stores comes from category pages, because shoppers search for types of product (women's trail running shoes) more often than for individual items. Product pages capture specific searches and brand-plus-model queries. Buying guides and comparisons catch shoppers earlier. Ecommerce SEO aligns each of these page types with the searches it should win.

Category pages that rank and sell

Strong category pages combine a clear heading, a short introduction that helps shoppers choose, well-organised filters, internal links to sub-categories and a few helpful answers lower on the page. They avoid long blocks of keyword-heavy text above the products. I review the category structure against search demand, add or merge categories where needed and improve the content on the ones that matter most.

Product pages with unique, useful content

Manufacturer descriptions appear on hundreds of sites, so copying them adds nothing. Product pages perform better with original descriptions, specifications, sizing guidance, real photos, reviews and answers to common questions. For large catalogues, I prioritise top-selling and high-margin products first and create templates that make good content easier to produce at scale.

Faceted navigation and crawl control

Filters for size, colour, price and brand can create millions of URL combinations. Google's documentation on faceted navigation recommends preventing crawling of unhelpful combinations while allowing a few valuable ones, such as a popular brand-plus-category filter, to be indexed as proper landing pages. Getting this right saves crawl budget and stops duplicate pages competing with categories.

Shopify SEO specifics

Shopify is a solid platform for SEO, but it has quirks: fixed URL prefixes such as /collections/ and /products/, duplicate product URLs inside collection paths, limited robots.txt control without editing the robots template, and apps that add heavy scripts. Shopify SEO work covers theme speed, canonical handling, structured data, collection structure and app audits. WooCommerce, Magento, BigCommerce and custom builds have their own checklists.

Product structured data and merchant listings

Product markup with price, availability, ratings and shipping or return details can make listings eligible for richer results and helps Google understand product information. It must match what is visible on the page. Combining structured data with a Merchant Center feed gives Google consistent, up-to-date product data across search surfaces.

Out-of-stock, discontinued and seasonal products

Temporarily out-of-stock products should usually stay live with clear availability and alternatives. Permanently discontinued products can be redirected to the closest replacement or category, or kept if they still attract links and searches. Seasonal categories, such as sale or holiday gift pages, work best on stable URLs that are updated each year.

Content that supports buying decisions

Buying guides, size guides, comparisons and care instructions answer the questions shoppers ask before purchasing. They earn links, support category pages through internal links and reduce returns. The SEO content services page explains how this content is planned.

Ecommerce SEO for international stores

Stores selling in several countries need correct currencies, shipping details and hreflang between country versions. The international SEO service covers that setup. A related example of an online brand's search work is in the FeetFinder case study.

Measuring ecommerce SEO

I track organic revenue and conversion rate by page type, category rankings, indexed product counts and crawl health. Reporting separates brand and non-brand traffic, so you can see whether SEO is winning new customers rather than just capturing people who already know you.

Site speed for large catalogues

Online stores often carry heavy themes, large images, tracking scripts and apps. Each adds weight, and slow category and product pages hurt both conversion and page experience. I measure real-user data from Search Console and analytics, then fix the biggest causes first: image sizing and formats, unused apps, render-blocking scripts and slow server responses. The goal is fast pages for shoppers on mid-range phones, not a perfect lab score.

Internal linking across the catalogue

Breadcrumbs, sub-category links, related products, recently viewed items and links from guides all help search engines reach deep pages and understand the hierarchy. Products buried many clicks from the homepage are crawled less often. A clear internal linking plan keeps important categories and products close to the top.

Ecommerce SEO and paid search together

Organic and paid search work best when they share data. Paid search reveals which queries convert quickly; organic work can then target them with stronger category pages, reducing dependence on ads over time for non-brand terms.
