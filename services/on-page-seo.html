On-Page SEO

On page SEO services that help existing pages match intent and rank

On page SEO services improve the pages you already have: matching each page to one search intent, then tightening titles, headings, content, internal links, images and structured data.

Get a free SEO auditBook a call

What on page SEO services change

On-page SEO is everything you control on the page itself. It overlaps with content and technical SEO, but its focus is narrow and practical: making sure each important page targets the right query, answers it better than competing pages and sends clear signals to search engines and readers.

  • Title links written for the query and the click, without stuffing
  • Meta descriptions that summarise the page accurately
  • One clear H1 and a logical heading structure
  • Content gaps filled based on what searchers actually need
  • Internal links with descriptive anchor text
  • Images with descriptive file names and alt text
  • Structured data that matches visible content

Starting with search intent

Before editing a word, I check what Google currently ranks for the target query. If the results are guides and the page is a sales page, or the results are product listings and the page is a blog post, no amount of optimisation will close the gap. Matching the format and angle searchers expect is the single biggest on-page lever.

Fixing keyword cannibalisation

When several pages target the same query, search engines may rotate between them and none ranks well. On-page work includes mapping one primary keyword to each page, differentiating overlapping pages, consolidating where two pages are truly the same and pointing internal links at the preferred page.

Internal linking as an on-page lever

Internal links help search engines discover pages and understand how they relate. A page with few internal links pointing to it often struggles, however good its content. I add contextual links from relevant pages using descriptive anchors, connect cluster pages to their main service page, and make sure no important page is orphaned.

Writing for people, not keyword density

Google's guidance is clear that keyword stuffing is spam and that content should be written for people. Primary keywords belong in the title, the main heading and naturally in the opening, but the rest of the page should use the language your customers use: related terms, examples, specifics and answers. That is also what makes content useful to AI search features.

How on page SEO fits with other services

On-page work gives the fastest results on pages that already have some visibility. If pages are not indexed at all, start with technical SEO services. If topics are missing entirely, SEO content services plan and write new pages. If pages are strong but lack authority, link building services are the next step.

What a typical on page SEO month includes

A monthly on-page engagement usually covers a defined set of priority pages. For each one, I review the current rankings and the search results, write a short brief of changes, update titles, headings, copy, internal links and images, and track the outcome in Search Console over the following weeks. Pages that respond well become templates for similar pages; pages that do not are reviewed again for intent or competition.

Changes are documented so your team knows exactly what was edited and why, and nothing is changed on live pages without your approval.

On page SEO for different page types

Service pages need clear explanations, proof and a next step. Product and category pages need accurate specifications, helpful descriptions and clean filtering. Blog articles need direct answers, depth and links to related services. Location pages need genuine local detail. Each type has its own checklist, but every one starts with the intent of the query and the needs of the reader.

Before and after tracking

Every change is dated in a log, so movements in Search Console can be connected to specific edits. I compare clicks, impressions, average position and click-through rate for several weeks before and after each update, allowing for seasonality. That evidence shows which changes work on your site, rather than relying on generic best practice.

Who it is for

Who on-page SEO is for

  • Sites that rank on page two or three for terms they should own
  • Service pages written for the company, not for the searcher
  • Blogs with traffic that never turns into enquiries
  • Teams that publish often but have no keyword-to-page map
  • Businesses with several pages competing for the same keyword

Signs you need it

  • High impressions, low click-through rate in Search Console
  • Two or more pages swapping positions for one query
  • Titles cut off or rewritten by Google
  • Pages with no internal links pointing to them
  • Copy that never mentions the terms buyers actually use
