SaaS & B2B SEO

B2B SEO and SaaS SEO that turn organic search into pipeline

B2B SEO helps SaaS and B2B companies reach buyers throughout long research cycles, with content and pages that answer real buying questions and lead to demos, trials and qualified pipeline.

Get a free SEO auditBook a call

Why B2B SEO is different

B2B purchases involve several people, longer research and higher stakes

Searches are fewer but more valuable, and buyers often search for problems, comparisons, integrations and pricing long before they speak to sales. B2B SEO focuses on those moments, measuring success by pipeline rather than raw traffic.

The page types that drive SaaS pipeline

Product and feature pages aligned with how buyers describe the problem

Use-case and industry pages

Honest comparison and alternative pages

Integration pages for the tools buyers already use

Pricing pages that answer common questions

Templates, calculators and free tools

Expert articles on problems your product solves

Each type serves a different stage and links to the next logical step.

Bottom-of-funnel first

For most SaaS companies, the fastest return comes from high-intent searches: [category] software for [industry], [competitor] alternatives, [tool] integration.

These searches have lower volume but convert well. Educational content comes next, building authority and capturing buyers earlier.

Comparison pages done honestly

Comparison and alternative pages work when they are accurate, fair and current.

Explain who each product suits, show real differences and update the page when competitors change. Misleading comparisons damage trust with sophisticated buyers and can create legal risk.

Content with real expertise

B2B buyers can tell when content is written by someone who has never done the job.

The best content draws on your product team, customer success and subject experts: real workflows, screenshots, data and opinions. I run short interviews with your experts and turn them into content that reads like it came from practitioners.

Technical SEO for SaaS sites

SaaS sites often combine a marketing site, documentation, a help centre, a blog and app subdomains, sometimes on different platforms.

Common issues include JavaScript rendering, duplicated docs versions, unindexed help content and app pages leaking into the index. I review the full setup so the right pages are crawlable and the rest stay out of search.

Authority and digital PR for B2B

Original research, benchmark reports, partner integrations, podcasts, conference talks and customer stories earn links and mentions from industry publications.

These also influence how AI assistants describe your category and which vendors they recommend.

SaaS SEO and AI search

Buyers increasingly ask assistants for software recommendations

Consistent positioning across your site, review platforms and industry coverage helps assistants describe your product accurately. The AI search optimization service covers this in more depth.

Measuring B2B SEO

I connect organic sessions to sign-ups, demo requests, trials and opportunities in your CRM where possible, separate brand from non-brand growth and report on influenced pipeline as well as last-click conversions.

That gives leadership a realistic view of SEO's contribution.

Early-stage or scaling?

This page focuses on SaaS and B2B companies with an established product and site.

Pre-seed and seed companies setting up foundations will find the startup SEO page more relevant, and agencies serving B2B clients can use white label SEO.

Keyword research for B2B buyers

B2B search volumes look small in keyword tools, and many valuable queries show little or no volume at all.

I combine tool data with sales call notes, support tickets, CRM lost-deal reasons and the language customers use in reviews. That reveals the questions buyers actually ask and the terms they use, which are often different from internal product names.

A typical B2B SEO quarter

The first quarter usually covers a technical review, keyword and page mapping, two or three high-intent pages such as comparisons or integrations, and the first expert-led articles.

Later quarters expand clusters, refresh content and build authority through research and partnerships. Progress is reviewed with marketing and sales leaders so priorities follow the pipeline.

Working with sales and customer success

The best B2B SEO ideas often come from customer-facing teams

A short monthly exchange with sales and customer success surfaces new objections, competitor mentions and use cases worth covering, and lets those teams use new content in their conversations.

It also keeps messaging consistent across the website, sales decks and outreach.

Who it is for

  • B2B service firms with long sales cycles
  • Teams measured on pipeline, not traffic

Signs you need it

  • Organic signups are not tracked to revenue
