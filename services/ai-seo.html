Overview

What AI search optimization is

AI search optimization, also called AI SEO, answer engine optimization (AEO) or generative engine optimization (GEO), is the work of making your brand and content easy for AI-driven search features to find, understand, trust and cite. It builds on traditional SEO rather than replacing it: Google states that the same fundamentals apply to AI features in Search, and pages must be indexed and eligible to be shown in search to appear as supporting links.

How AI assistants choose what to show

AI systems generate answers from their training data and, increasingly, from live search results.

They tend to draw on content that directly answers the question, comes from sources that are frequently mentioned and trusted on the topic, and clearly identifies who is behind it. Brands that are consistently described across their own site, reviews, directories and third-party publications are easier for these systems to recognise.

Content that AI systems can use

Pages that open sections with a direct answer, use descriptive headings, include specifics such as prices, steps, definitions and comparisons, and cite their own experience are easier to quote.

Vague marketing copy is hard to summarise accurately. This is the same advice that makes content better for people, which is why it also helps traditional rankings.

Crawler access and robots.txt choices

Different AI companies use different crawlers

Some fetch pages for live answers; others collect training data.

I review your robots.txt and server rules so the crawlers you want can access your public pages, and explain the trade-offs of blocking any of them.

Blocking Googlebot would affect both normal search and AI features in Google Search.

Measuring AI visibility honestly

There is no complete equivalent of Search Console for AI assistants yet, and answers vary by user and session.

I track a fixed set of questions across assistants on a regular schedule, record whether and how your brand appears, and combine that with referral traffic from AI tools in analytics. Results are reported as trends rather than guarantees.

Common AI search problems I fix

Assistants describing a company with outdated services or prices, confusing it with a similarly named business, recommending competitors for questions the brand should own, or failing to mention the brand at all are the most common issues.

The causes are usually inconsistent information across the web, thin or vague pages, missing third-party coverage or technical barriers. Each has a practical fix, which is why the work starts with testing what assistants say today.
