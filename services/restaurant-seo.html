Overview

How diners choose a restaurant online

Diners search with a mix of cuisine, location and occasion: sushi near me, best brunch in [area], Italian restaurant with outdoor seating, private dining room [city]. They compare photos, ratings, menus and prices in a few seconds.

Restaurant SEO makes sure your restaurant appears for these searches and that the profile and website persuade them to choose you.

Restaurant SEO starts with your Google Business Profile

For most restaurants, the profile drives more visits than the website

It should have the right cuisine category, accurate hours including holidays, a menu link, reservation and ordering links, attributes such as outdoor seating or vegan options, plenty of recent photos of food, interior and exterior, and replies to reviews.

Keeping it current is the single most valuable restaurant SEO task.

Menus as web pages, not PDFs

PDF and image menus are hard to read on phones and hard for search engines to understand.

A text-based menu page with dish names, short descriptions, prices and dietary labels helps diners decide and helps the restaurant appear for dish-specific searches. Menu structured data can be added where it matches the visible menu.

Pages for occasions and services

Private dining, events, catering, brunch, set menus, gift vouchers and seasonal menus each deserve their own page if you offer them.

These pages rank for specific searches with high intent and often bring larger bookings. Restaurants that also cater can see the catering SEO page for that side of the business.

Reviews and reputation across platforms

Diners check Google, TripAdvisor, Yelp and delivery apps

Encourage reviews through table cards, receipts and follow-up messages for bookings, and reply to every review politely. Never offer discounts in exchange for reviews; it breaks platform rules.

Delivery apps and your own website

Delivery platforms bring orders but take commission and often outrank restaurants for their own name.

Making sure your website ranks first for your brand, with a direct ordering link where you have one, keeps more revenue. Accurate details across platforms avoid confusing customers.

Local content and press

Local food bloggers, city guides, newspapers and event listings provide mentions and links that strengthen local prominence.

Chef stories, sourcing from local producers and community events give journalists something to write about.

Restaurant website essentials

Fast mobile pages with the menu, hours and location one tap away

Reservation or ordering buttons visible on every page

Real photography of dishes and the dining room

Clear directions, parking and accessibility information

Restaurant schema with address, hours, cuisine and menu link

Multi-location restaurant groups

Groups need a location page for each restaurant with its own menu, hours, photos, reviews and booking link, plus a separate profile for each.

Central management keeps details consistent while letting each site show its personality.

Keeping details accurate everywhere

Hours, menus and phone numbers change, and outdated details on any platform cost bookings.

A simple monthly check of the profile, website, reservation platform, delivery apps and major directories keeps everything aligned, with special hours updated before holidays.

Restaurant SEO for new openings

A new restaurant can build anticipation before opening: a website with the concept, menu preview and opening date, a profile set up with the opening date where Google allows it, local press outreach and social posts linking to the site.

That way, searches for the name return useful information from day one.

Measuring restaurant SEO

Useful measures include profile views, calls, direction requests, menu views, reservation and ordering clicks, website visits and, where possible, covers booked online.

Comparing these month by month, while allowing for seasonality, shows what is working.

Restaurant SEO by market

Diners in the UK often check TripAdvisor and Google together; in the US, Yelp and OpenTable matter; in Australia, Google and local food guides; in the UAE, Zomato, Talabat and Google, with many searches in English and Arabic.

The listings you prioritise depend on where your diners look.

Dietary and accessibility information

Diners with allergies, dietary needs or mobility requirements search specifically for restaurants that can accommodate them.

Clear menu labels, an allergen policy, accessibility details and attributes on the profile help these diners choose you with confidence.
