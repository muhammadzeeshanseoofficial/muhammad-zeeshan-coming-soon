Overview

What safe link building services look like

Links remain one of the ways search engines discover pages and judge how prominent a site is. Google's spam policies also describe link schemes clearly: buying or selling links that pass ranking credit, excessive link exchanges, large-scale guest posting with keyword-rich anchors and automated link creation. Off-page work that relies on those tactics carries a real risk of being ignored or penalised.

My approach focuses on links a site would earn if it were genuinely useful and well promoted. That takes longer than buying links, but it builds authority that lasts.

Anchor text and natural link profiles

Natural links mostly use brand names, page titles and plain phrases

A profile full of exact-match keyword anchors is a warning sign. I do not ask publishers for specific keyword anchors, and internal linking handles descriptive anchors on your own site.

Building assets worth linking to

The most efficient link building starts with something worth citing

That could be an original survey, a dataset from your own business, a clear explainer that becomes the go-to reference, or a free tool. I help plan the asset, then promote it to the people most likely to reference it.

Off-page SEO beyond links

Off-page signals also include reviews, brand searches, mentions in industry publications and consistent business information across the web.

For AI search, being mentioned and cited by trusted sources helps assistants recognise your brand as an authority, which is covered in AI search optimization.

When link building is not the right first step

If key pages are not indexed or do not match search intent, links will not fix them.

A short review of technical and on-page health comes first, so new authority flows to pages that can rank.

Measuring link building honestly

Link counts alone say little

I track the number of relevant referring domains earned, the pages they link to, referral traffic, brand mentions and, over time, the rankings of the pages that received new links. Third-party authority scores are useful for comparison, but they are not Google metrics, so they are reported as context rather than goals.

Link building for different types of business

A local service business benefits most from local press, associations, sponsorships and suppliers.

An online store benefits from product reviews by independent publishers, gift guides and brand collaborations.

A B2B or SaaS company benefits from original research, integrations and partner pages.

The tactics change, but the rule is the same: links should come from places your customers and peers actually read.

Before outreach starts, I agree a list of target topics and publications with you, so every placement fits your brand and nothing is published in places you would not want to be associated with.

Recovering from risky past link building

Some sites come to me after earlier providers bought links or built networks of low-quality guest posts.

I review the profile, identify patterns that look manipulative, remove what can be removed and use the disavow tool only where it is genuinely justified, such as after a manual action. Then earned links gradually replace the risky ones.

How outreach is done

Outreach is personal and relevant

Each message explains why the resource helps that publication's readers, is sent to the right person and is followed up once at most.

I keep records of every contact, avoid mass templates and never promise payment for links.

Fewer, better pitches build relationships with editors that keep producing coverage over time.
