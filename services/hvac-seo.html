Overview

How customers search for HVAC services

HVAC searches fall into two groups. Urgent searches such as AC repair near me, furnace not working or emergency heating repair come from people who want someone today and usually call one of the first businesses they see in the map pack. Planned searches such as heat pump installation cost, best air conditioner for a small house or HVAC maintenance plan involve research and comparison. HVAC SEO covers both, with fast local visibility for urgent jobs and helpful pages for planned ones.

The pages an HVAC website needs

A page for each core service: AC repair, AC installation, furnace or boiler repair, heat pumps, ductwork, indoor air quality, maintenance plans

Emergency service page with clear hours and response information

Service-area pages for the towns you genuinely cover, with local detail

Brands and equipment you install and service

Financing, rebates and warranties where they apply

Reviews, licences, insurance and certifications

Each page answers the questions customers ask before calling: what is included, typical costs or how quotes work, how fast you can come and why to trust you.

Google Business Profile for HVAC companies

Most urgent HVAC calls start in the map pack

The profile should use the most accurate primary category, list every service, show real photos of vans, technicians and installations, keep hours accurate (including emergency availability) and collect reviews steadily.

Service-area businesses should hide the address if customers do not visit

Full details are on the Google Business Profile optimization page.

Seasonal planning

Demand for air conditioning peaks in hot months and heating in cold months, with maintenance searches in spring and autumn.

Pages should be updated and promoted ahead of each season, because SEO changes take weeks to show. Starting in the quieter months gives the best return when the rush arrives.

Service-area pages without doorway content

HVAC companies often serve many towns

Creating a near-identical page for each one breaks Google's doorway page guidance and rarely ranks.

Better pages include jobs completed in that area, local housing types and common problems, response times, reviews from local customers and photos.

Start with the towns that bring the most valuable work.

Content that answers homeowner questions

Articles on why an AC is blowing warm air, how often to replace filters, heat pump versus furnace costs, or what a maintenance visit includes attract homeowners before they call.

They build trust and link naturally to service pages. They should be written or reviewed by experienced technicians, with safety advice where relevant.

Reviews and reputation

Reviews influence local rankings and whether people call

Ask after every completed job, ideally by text with a direct link, and reply to every review. Mentioning the service and town naturally in replies is fine; stuffing keywords is not.

Tracking calls and booked jobs

HVAC SEO is measured by calls, form leads and booked jobs, not rankings alone.

Call tracking with number swapping on the website, UTM tags on the profile link and a simple way for dispatchers to log the source of each job give a clear picture of what SEO brings in.

HVAC SEO in different markets

Terms vary by country: HVAC and AC repair in the US, heating engineer, boiler repair and air conditioning installation in the UK, furnace repair in Canada, air conditioning service and split system installation in Australia and AC maintenance in the UAE, where cooling demand is year-round.

Common HVAC SEO mistakes

Using a single page for every service, listing dozens of cities in the footer, hiding prices and phone numbers, publishing generic articles copied from manufacturer sites and ignoring reviews are common.

So is a slow website full of heavy sliders that frustrates people with a broken AC on a hot day. Fixing these basics often brings more calls before any new content is written.

A typical first three months

Month one covers the audit, profile fixes, tracking and the most important service pages.

Month two focuses on emergency and installation pages, service-area pages for priority towns and a review routine.

Month three adds seasonal content and local links, such as supplier pages, trade associations and community sponsorships.

Results are reviewed monthly against calls and booked jobs.
