What Is Generative Engine Optimization?

Generative Engine Optimization, often called GEO, is the work of helping generative systems use your content as a source when they write or assemble an answer.

Those systems may combine several pages, summarise them, and show a smaller set of citations than a classic results list.

The page that is easy to retrieve, easy to interpret and easy to attribute has a better chance of being one of those sources.

GEO therefore includes technical access, content quality, entity and identity signals, and a realistic view of what you can influence.

It is not a promise that a model will always name you, and it is not a replacement for ordinary SEO.

It is a way to brief a team when the result the customer sees is a generated answer with citations.

The question What Is Generative Engine Optimization deserves a definition first, then a method, which is the shape of this page. If you only needed a slogan, you would not need a guide.

How GEO Relates to AEO and Traditional SEO

AEO focuses on making a page answer-ready. GEO focuses on being a usable source inside a generated composition.

In practice the work overlaps, and a team should not split into two rival programmes.

Traditional SEO remains the foundation, because a page that is blocked, duplicated or thin is a weak source for any system.

If you must brief the work in layers, start with crawl and index, then with unique pages, then with answer structure and identity.

The labels help when they prevent a client from thinking that old technical problems no longer matter. The labels hurt when they become a reason to buy a new tool and skip the pages.

This site keeps a definition for each term and then points to one practical plan. You can use the terms in a meeting and still ship the same kind of good work.

What Generative Systems Need From a Source

A system that composes an answer needs to fetch or retrieve a passage, judge whether it is relevant, and decide whether it is trustworthy enough to use.

Your job is to make those steps less error-prone, not to game a hidden score you have never seen.

Passages should make sense when they are lifted out of the page, which is why short, complete answers matter.

The site should say who wrote the page and who stands behind it, with consistent names and a reachable about or organisation page.

The content should add something a model cannot just invent, such as a specific method, a scoped process or a documented constraint. Generic commentary that could live on any marketing blog is a weak source.

Freshness matters when the facts change, and unchanging basics should not be rewritten only to look new. A stable, accurate page is easier to reuse than a page that changes its definition every month.

Preparing a Page for Generative Use

Give the page one intent, a short answer, a clear outline and visible support for the claims you make. Keep the unique content in HTML that can be rendered without a special user action.

Add authorship and organisation information that matches the rest of the site. Use structured data only when it describes the visible page, and keep dates honest.

Link to related pages that own neighbouring questions, so a system that follows citations can find the deeper source. Avoid doorway pages that repeat the same answer with a different city or synonym in the title.

Those pages are weak sources and they weaken each other. A smaller set of complete pages is easier to trust than a large set of near duplicates.

Identity, Entities and Attribution

Generative systems are more useful to you when they can attach a passage to a real source.

Use one consistent name for the person and the site, and repeat that identity on the author byline, the about page and the organisation markup you already support.

Explain the person’s relevant experience in a factual way, without invented titles or awards. If the business serves specific markets, say so clearly on the market pages rather than implying a global presence you do not have.

Entity SEO, which has its own guide, goes deeper on this identity work. For GEO, the practical point is that a floating article with no author and no organisation is harder to attribute.

Do not stuff organisation names into every sentence. Put them where a reader would expect to see who is speaking.

A Worked Example of a Source Page That Was Hard to Use

A thought-leadership article opened with metaphors, mixed three topics, and placed the only concrete advice in a downloadable file. A generative system that could only read the HTML would have almost nothing accurate to quote.

The rewrite kept the same URL, added a short definition, moved the advice into visible HTML, and cut the extra topics into links.

The author name was added to the byline, and the about page was linked from the end of the article.

The page became less decorative and more usable as a source. It also became easier for a human being to read, which is not an accident.

Pages that help a system often help a person first. That is a good test when a GEO proposal starts to sound abstract.

What You Can and Cannot Measure

You can measure whether your pages are indexable, whether they state answers, and whether your identity is consistent. You can watch for citations and mentions you can verify, and for questions in Search Console that your pages should own.

You cannot honestly promise a weekly count of every appearance inside every generative product. Those products change, and many do not provide a complete public log to the sources they use.

A practical report therefore talks about the work shipped and the evidence you can show, not about a fabricated share of voice. If a vendor offers exact generative tracking, ask how the data is collected and what it cannot see.

Be conservative in anything you later have to defend to a client. GEO is still young enough that overclaiming is a professional risk.

Responsible Limits

Do not write pages whose only job is to be ingested by a model, with no value for a person who visits.

Do not hide instructions or keyword piles that a person would never need, in the hope that a model will read them.

Do not impersonate another source, and do not invent credentials. Do not scrape unique material from a competitor and rewrite it as your expertise.

The same ethics that apply to ordinary SEO apply here, with a little more temptation to fool a machine. A page that is honest, specific and useful is the durable strategy.

It is also the strategy that will still make sense if a particular generative product changes tomorrow. That is the standard this guide is written to.

A Sensible Starting Plan

Begin with technical access on the pages you already care about, then improve the short answers and identity on those URLs.

Add or refresh a small set of distinctive guides that only you could have written in that form, because they describe your method.

Connect them in a cluster, and make sure the about and service pages support the same person and organisation names. Review the set on a quarterly cycle, which is the job of the GEO plan guide on this site.

Resist a complete content reboot that leaves you with fifty new thin pages and no better answers. GEO work should shrink confusion, not expand the pile of URLs.

If you need help, brief the work as part of content and technical SEO, not as a mysterious add-on. The shipping unit is still a better page.

GEO Mistakes to Avoid

Do not rename your entire SEO programme and then change nothing on the pages. Do not buy links or citations that you would be embarrassed to explain.

Do not treat a chatbot’s answer today as a permanent ranking report. Do not flood the site with machine-written pages that all say the same thing in different order.

Do not ignore robots and rendering issues because the conversation has moved to AI. Do not hide the author’s name or the organisation if you want attribution.

Do not mix the AEO and GEO definitions on one thin page just to collect both phrases. Give each idea a proper URL, then link them.

Related Guides and the Practical Work

Read the AEO guide for the answer-page pattern, the entity guide for identity, and the GEO plan guide for a quarterly sequence. Read the technical and content guides when the issue is crawl, structure or writing.

Those pages together are the programme. This page’s job was to define Generative Engine Optimization without dressing it up as a secret.

If the definition is clear, you can brief a team and start on a real URL today. Pick a page that already almost answers an important question, and make it easier to retrieve, interpret and attribute.

Then look at the next question in the same cluster. That is GEO as a practice, not as a slogan.
