Overview

Where Hotel SEO fits alongside the booking platforms

Most independent hotels rely heavily on online travel agencies. They bring visibility, but commission on every booking adds up, and the guest relationship belongs to the platform.

Hotel SEO does not replace those channels; it grows the share of guests who find and book you directly.

That starts with the searches that already include your name, which OTAs often target with ads, and extends to searches about your location, such as boutique hotel near [landmark] or hotel with parking in [area].

Room pages with real photos, amenities and policies, a location page that answers how to get there and what is nearby, and a fast, trustworthy booking engine are the foundation.

Your Google Business Profile and Google's hotel results are just as important as your website. Accurate amenities, photos, reviews and connected booking links influence whether travellers choose your site or a platform.

Technical health matters too. Booking engines on separate domains, slow image-heavy pages and duplicate content from channel managers often hold hotel websites back, which is where technical SEO work helps.

Making direct booking worth it for guests

Search visibility alone does not move bookings away from platforms; guests need a reason to book direct.

Many independent hotels offer small but meaningful benefits, such as flexible cancellation, breakfast, room upgrades when available or late checkout. State them clearly on the homepage, room pages and booking engine, and keep them consistent with what the platforms show.

Trust signals matter as much as price

Secure checkout, recognisable payment options, clear policies, up-to-date photos and recent reviews reassure guests who might otherwise default to a platform they know.

Measure what happens after changes

If direct share rises but average booking value falls, adjust offers rather than adding discounts by default.

Travellers from different countries search differently: UK guests often look for B&Bs and inns, US guests for boutique hotels and resorts, and UAE searches include many international languages.

Properties in Dubai, London, Paris or Rome face intense competition and benefit most from niche and location-led content.

How results are measured

Track direct bookings, revenue and average booking value from organic search and Google hotel links, alongside the commission saved compared with OTA bookings.

Technical problems that hold hotel websites back

Hotel websites often have problems that are invisible to owners: booking engines on a separate domain that breaks tracking, multiple language versions without hreflang, very large images that slow mobile pages, and duplicate room descriptions copied from channel managers.

Each reduces rankings or conversions.

A focused technical review identifies which of these affect your property and fixes them in order of impact.

Often the quickest win is image compression and a lighter mobile homepage, followed by clean language versions for key source markets.
