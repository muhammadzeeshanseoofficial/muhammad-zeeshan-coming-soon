Nonprofit SEO for charities that want to reach more people

Nonprofit SEO helps charities and nonprofits reach the people they serve, attract volunteers and donors, and make their expertise easier to find, using clear content and a well-structured website.

Get a free SEO auditBook a call

Who nonprofits need to reach through search

Nonprofits serve several audiences at once. People seeking help search for support, advice and services. Volunteers search for opportunities nearby. Donors search for causes and organisations to support. Journalists and partners search for expertise. Nonprofit SEO plans pages for each of these audiences rather than relying on a single homepage.

Many nonprofits have deep expertise that can help people, such as guides, advice and research. Publishing it as clear, accessible web pages, rather than PDFs, helps more people find it and builds the organisation's authority.

Local nonprofits benefit from a complete Google Business Profile optimization and local pages. National organisations benefit from strong topic pages and structured information about services.

Churches and faith groups with similar aims can also see the church SEO page.

Content ideas that attract nonprofits and charities customers

Helpful guides bring in people earlier in their research and support your service pages with internal links. These topics come from the questions customers of nonprofits and charities commonly ask:

  • Advice guides on your cause
  • How to get help pages
  • Volunteer role guides
  • Impact reports as web pages
  • Fundraising event pages
  • Stories of people supported
  • Campaign pages
  • FAQ about donations

What a high-converting nonprofit page includes

Ranking is only half the job; the page also has to turn a visitor into an enquiry. Every key page I build for this industry includes:

  • Clear routes for each audience
  • Prominent donate button
  • Volunteer sign-up
  • Help information
  • Trust details such as registration
  • Accessible design

A 90-day nonprofit seo plan

  1. Audience mapping. Beneficiaries, volunteers, donors, partners.
  2. Site structure. Clear sections for each audience.
  3. Advice content. Expert guides as web pages.
  4. Technical basics. Speed, accessibility, HTTPS.
  5. Local visibility. Profiles and local pages.

Nonprofit seo across the US, UK, Canada, Australia and the UAE

Terms differ by country: charity is common in the UK and Australia, nonprofit or non-profit in the US and Canada, and NGO in India and the UAE. Donation regulations, tax relief language and directories such as charity registers also vary, so content must match the audience's country.

How results are measured

Track donations, volunteer sign-ups, enquiries and help requests from organic search, plus visibility for advice content. Report these figures to trustees alongside other channels so the value of organic search is understood and resourced. Review the numbers every quarter and adjust priorities.

Accessible advice that helps people

People seeking support often search in stressful situations, frequently on mobile. Advice pages should be written plainly, structured with clear headings, accessible to screen readers and quick to load. Where content touches on health, finance or legal matters, show who wrote or reviewed it and when it was last updated, as accuracy matters to both readers and search engines.

Donation pages that build trust

Donors want to know where money goes. Clear donation pages explaining impact in honest terms, showing registration details, offering secure payment and answering common questions about tax relief or gift aid where relevant convert better and can rank for supportive searches.

Volunteer recruitment

Separate pages for volunteer roles, with locations, time commitment, requirements and how to apply, rank for local volunteer searches. Keep them updated and remove filled roles or mark them closed.

Research and campaigns

Reports and campaign pages earn links from news outlets and partners. Publish key findings as web pages with clear summaries and data, linking to downloadable reports. Keep campaign URLs stable so they build authority over time.

Stories with consent

Stories of people helped bring your work to life. Share them with informed consent, protecting privacy, and use them to show impact on service and donation pages.

Working with small teams and volunteers

Many nonprofits have limited staff. A simple content calendar, templates and clear ownership of updates keep the website current without overwhelming the team.

Structuring a site for several audiences

Clear top navigation such as Get help, Volunteer, Donate and About lets each audience find its path quickly. Each section should have its own landing page explaining options and linking to detailed pages. This structure helps both visitors and search engines understand the site, and stops donor content crowding out help for people in need.

Test the structure with real users from each audience; even a handful of short sessions reveals where people get stuck.

Events and fundraising pages

Runs, walks, dinners and challenge events attract participants searching for local events. Keep a stable page for recurring events, update dates each year and add results and photos afterwards, so the page builds authority rather than starting again each year.
