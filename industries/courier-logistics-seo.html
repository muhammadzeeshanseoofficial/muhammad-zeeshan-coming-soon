Logistics SEO for couriers, freight and 3PL companies

Logistics SEO helps courier, freight and third-party logistics companies get found by shippers, e-commerce brands and procurement teams researching providers, and turns that research into quote requests.

Get a free SEO auditBook a call

How logistics SEO supports B2B sales

Logistics buyers rarely buy from a single search, but they use search to build shortlists.

A shipper might search for same-day courier [city], refrigerated freight UK to Europe or 3PL for e-commerce, then compare providers on coverage, capabilities, reliability and how easy it is to get a quote. Logistics SEO puts your company in those results and makes the comparison easy.

The structure matters

Service pages for each mode (courier, same-day, LTL, FTL, freight forwarding, warehousing, fulfilment), industry pages for the sectors you serve, and coverage or lane pages for your main routes match how buyers search. Each page should answer capability questions directly: vehicle types, temperature control, tracking, insurance, cut-off times and integrations.

Many logistics websites are thin and generic, which is an opportunity

Clear, specific content often ranks quickly in this industry.

For logistics software or platforms, the SaaS and B2B SEO page covers the product side.

Writing industry pages that speak to shippers

A pharmaceutical distributor, an online fashion brand and a construction supplier all need logistics, but they worry about different things: temperature control and compliance, returns and peak capacity, or heavy loads and site deliveries.

Industry pages that address those concerns in the buyer's language convert far better than a generic list of services.

Each industry page should describe typical shipments, the specific challenges, how your services solve them, relevant certifications and a short example where the client agrees.

Link to the service pages involved.

These pages also rank for searches like logistics for e-commerce or construction logistics, which generic service pages rarely capture.

Logistics seo across the US, UK, Canada, Australia and the UAE

UK and European buyers search for haulage, couriers and freight forwarding; US buyers for trucking, LTL and 3PL; Australian buyers for freight and transport companies; UAE buyers for freight forwarding and logistics in Dubai and Jebel Ali.

Content should use the terms buyers in each market use.

How results are measured

Track quote requests by page, quote-to-contract conversion and the value of contracts sourced from organic search over quarters.

Using case studies without breaking confidentiality

Logistics clients often prefer not to be named, but buyers still want proof.

Anonymised case studies work well: describe the type of business, the challenge, the solution and the outcome in general terms, with any figures the client has approved. A page about an e-commerce retailer that needed next-day delivery across the country, or a food producer that needed temperature-controlled distribution, shows capability without exposing anyone.

Link each case study to the relevant service and industry pages so buyers can move from proof to detail and then to a quote.

Technical basics logistics sites often miss

Logistics websites often hide important content behind tracking portals or PDFs, use slow templates and have little structured data.

Make sure service and industry content is in HTML, pages are fast on mobile, and location details are consistent across your site and profiles.

If you run multiple depots, give each its own location page and Google Business Profile with accurate hours and photos.

Drivers, customers and recruits all look for them.
