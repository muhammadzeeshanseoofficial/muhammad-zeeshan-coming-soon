Overview

What Solar SEO needs to answer before buyers call

Solar is a long, considered purchase. Buyers research payback, incentives, batteries, roof suitability and financing before they request a consultation.

Solar SEO builds content around those questions and links it to clear service pages for residential solar, commercial solar, battery storage and maintenance.

Incentives and rules change by country, state and utility, so content must be accurate, dated and updated. Link to official sources such as government energy agencies rather than making promises about savings.

Local proof, including installation galleries, reviews and certifications, helps you stand out from national lead sellers in your area.

Building trust in a category with aggressive sales tactics

Solar buyers are wary

Many have received cold calls, door knocks or online ads promising large savings, and they search to check whether those claims are real.

Solar SEO works best when your content is the calm, factual voice in that research: explaining how savings are calculated, what affects payback, how incentives work in your region and what questions to ask any installer, including you.

That kind of content earns links from local news, community groups and forums, and it gets cited by AI assistants answering solar questions.

It also pre-qualifies buyers, so the consultations you get are with people who understand the investment.

Avoid publishing exact savings promises or outdated incentive figures

Date your guides, review them when rules change and link to the relevant service page so readers know where to go next.

Local pages that actually help solar buyers

Solar is a strong case for genuinely useful local pages, because the facts really do change by place.

Incentives, utility export rules, permitting timelines, typical roof types and even sun hours vary between states, provinces and cities.

A local page that explains those details for a specific area, shows installations nearby and names the utilities involved helps the buyer and gives Google a reason to rank it.

Avoid the common shortcut of cloning one page and swapping the city name.

Those pages add nothing, and Google's spam policies treat them as doorway pages.

Start with the areas where you install most and where your crews are based, and add more only when you can say something specific about them.
